OUR ROV
Meet Nemo! This year’s ROV design centers on two main enclosures that balance the vehicle and help it move smoothly through the water. A Delrin and acrylic frame connects the thrusters, cameras, and gripper. Because the sides and bottom of the frame bear most of the load, we built them from strong Delrin and used more cost-effective acrylic for the enclosure supports. Our gripper is designed to handle a variety of objects, from basket stars and coral species to lobster pots. Two external cameras connected to a Raspberry Pi provide downward- and forward-facing views, using a simpler, more compatible operating system. We programmed six BlueRobotics thrusters to move the ROV up, down, forward, and backward, as well as control pitch and yaw. Each thruster operates within a PWM range of 1400 to 1600. The thrusters are controlled using Python 3.0 and an Arduino Mega. Compared with previous years’ four-thruster design, the two additional thrusters are intended to increase speed. The ServoCity gripper opens and closes with a PlayStation 4 controller, also programmed using Python 3.0 and an Arduino. For added safety, the electrical system is divided between two enclosures. If one floods, the other can help protect the rest of the system. This year, we focused on preventing leaks and making the robot more dependable. Team members learned the correct procedures for sealing the enclosures, helping us avoid mistakes made in previous years. Instead of hand-potting each penetrator in the enclosure endcaps, we used cobalt penetrators. These make pressure testing more effective and help reduce concerns about leaks. We also added safeguards inside the enclosures: SAC trauma pads to absorb stray water and silica packets to help manage moisture. A custom-designed tray raises and organizes the electrical equipment above these materials. The pads take up little space while providing additional protection in the event of a leak. As in previous years, we installed 3D-printed thruster shrouds to help protect wildlife and drivers. Our goal is to build an ROV that can operate in a variety of aquatic environments while remaining safe for all living things.
